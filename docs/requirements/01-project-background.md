# 01. Project Background and Context

## 1. Institutional Background: Haramaya University
Haramaya University (HU), established in 1954 in East Hararghe, Ethiopia, is one of the premier higher education and research institutions in East Africa. The university has evolved from an agricultural college into a comprehensive, multi-campus research university structured into multiple academic colleges, institutes, specialized research stations, and administrative directorates.

To fulfill its tripartite mission—Teaching-Learning, Research, and Community Engagement—the university operates an extensive scientific and technical infrastructure consisting of **over 90 to 150+ wet and dry laboratories**, distributed across several specialized colleges and institutes:

- **Haramaya Institute of Technology (HiT):** Hosts 22+ engineering laboratories and workshops, including Reaction Engineering, HPLC High-Tech Analysis, Geotechnical, Automotive, CNC, IC Engine, Food Processing, and Materials Testing laboratories.
- **College of Agriculture and Environmental Sciences (CAES):** Houses specialized soil science, plant pathology, animal nutrition, tissue culture, and agronomy research laboratories.
- **College of Computing and Informatics (CCI):** Operates 6+ high-density computing laboratories supporting Software Engineering, Computer Science, Information Systems, and IT.
- **College of Health and Medical Sciences (CHMS - Harar Campus):** Operates clinical pathology, medical microbiology, parasitology, and anatomy teaching laboratories.
- **College of Veterinary Medicine (CVM):** Houses veterinary parasitology, veterinary microbiology, and animal hospital diagnostic facilities.
- **College of Natural and Computational Sciences (CNCS):** Maintains undergraduate and postgraduate wet laboratories for chemistry, biology, physics, and geology.

## 2. Institutional Governance: Centralized vs. Decentralized Demarcation

### Decentralized Ownership & Custody
Laboratory spaces, day-to-day experimental sessions, and physical equipment custody are decentralized. Academic laboratories are owned and operated by their respective colleges, schools, and academic departments. Custody of instruments is held by Department Heads, appointed Laboratory Managers, and Technical Assistants (TAs).

### Centralized Standardization & Oversight: The ULMD
To prevent fragmentation and ensure scientific accuracy and safety across the university, Haramaya University established the **University Laboratories Management Directorate (ULMD)**, headquartered at the **Central Laboratory Building, Office Number 34** (Main Campus), led by Directorate leadership (Dr. Meseret Girma).

The ULMD is mandated to:
1. Promote practical teaching and quality research through supervision and training.
2. Ensure the proper, safe, and wise use of laboratory equipment and consumables.
3. Provide centralized maintenance, installation, and calibration for laboratory equipment.
4. Review departmental laboratory inventories at least once a quarter.
5. Operate the university’s shared Central Laboratories.

### Campus Utilities & Physical Plant: The EFMD
A separate directorate, the **Estate Development and Facilities Management Directorate (EFMD)** (under Mr. Selahdin Ahmed), oversees campus-wide physical infrastructure through five processes, including **Building and Machineries Operations and Maintenance (BMOM)**. While EFMD is responsible for macro campus infrastructure (central power feeds, primary plumbing, building shells), technical laboratory equipment maintenance is the direct responsibility of the ULMD's Laboratory Technology Unit.

## 3. Operational Motivation for the Digital System
Despite the vital role of laboratories at Haramaya University, asset management and maintenance tracking have historically relied on manual paper logbooks, disconnected spreadsheet files, and informal inter-departmental communications. This operational model leads to:
1. **Inventory Opacity:** Departmental inventories are reviewed quarterly on paper, leaving university leadership without real-time visibility into equipment availability.
2. **Untracked Cross-Lab Transfers:** Equipment borrowed between departments or transferred to central facilities often lacks formal chain-of-custody tracking.
3. **Delayed Corrective Maintenance:** Breakdowns are reported through physical memos to Central Lab Building Office 34, causing prolonged equipment downtime.
4. **Neglected Calibration Schedules:** Precision analytical instruments (e.g., spectrophotometers, analytical balances, autoclaves) lack automated calibration tracking, violating international quality standards.
5. **Procurement Duplication:** Departments occasionally submit purchase requisitions for equipment already sitting idle in an adjacent department.

The **Laboratory Asset Management and Maintenance Tracking System** is designed to eliminate these bottlenecks by establishing an authoritative, transparent digital registry and service lifecycle platform for all laboratory equipment across Haramaya University.
